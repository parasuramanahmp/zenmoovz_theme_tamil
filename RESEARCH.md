# E-commerce research notes → what went into ZENMOOVZ v7

Looked at how large sports retailers (Decathlon India in particular) and general e-commerce
guidance structure a storefront, then mapped the patterns onto this project.

| Pattern seen | Where it shows up here |
|---|---|
| Sports retailers organise the whole shop **by sport first**, not by brand or product type | Sport strip under the header (All, Badminton, Cricket, Football, Volleyball, Throwball, Basketball, Swimming). It replaces the old page links. |
| Search sits front and centre with suggestions | Wide search bar with rotating hints, live product matches, highlighted terms, keyboard navigation and trending searches |
| Delivery date matters before checkout; shoppers check by pincode | Location picker in the header, with the delivery window shown on the outlet card and product page |
| Home pages stack proven blocks: hero promos, best sellers, categories, brands, new arrivals | Home order: hero carousel → best sellers → shop by category → shop the outlet → shop by brand → new arrivals → stay in the game |
| Product pages convert on: clear price and savings, size or option picker, delivery estimate, specs, reviews, a sticky add-to-cart on mobile | Product page: option picker that must be chosen, pincode check, spec table, ratings, sticky mobile bar |
| Cross-sells ("complete the kit") lift basket size | Bundle strip on product pages and a "complete the kit" row in the cart |
| Visible trust signals near the buy button | Returns and genuine-product notes beside delivery on the product page; value props on home |
| Physical stores sell by walking aisles and picking things off racks | **Shop the outlet**: interactive racks where you tap an item to lift it off and see its shop card |
| Video increasingly drives discovery; shoppable video links what you see to what you can buy | **Stay in the game**: products appear on screen at set moments, with markers on the timeline |

Things deliberately *not* copied: a full-screen curtain on every page change (slows shopping), and heavy
mega-menus (the sport strip does the same job with one tap).

Sources consulted: Decathlon India overviews and CRO write-ups, plus general product-page best-practice
articles (Hackernoon, Shipup, Blueport, Process Creative). Use them as inspiration, not as spec.
